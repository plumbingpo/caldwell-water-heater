When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater

Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

Consider the vent first. Gas water heater exhaust carries carbon monoxide, so an undersized, badly sloped, or loose vent can spill it back into living space. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and there is a lot to get right. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. A move to tankless or a heat pump brings gas line sizing, new vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines into the job.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
