Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Star

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Star

Most water heater failures we see in Star start with what's in the water. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be a real factor: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping you may hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Age matters just as much. Plenty of homes in the area still have the original tank, and once a tank is past about ten years, the anode rod has usually been consumed and rust starts working on the steel itself. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Setup matters too. A home on a private well can see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless unit in hard water should get annual descaling. And an older install getting a new heater may need updates it never had: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. All of these is fixable, and most cost far less to address early.
