Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater (leave first, and call the gas utility)
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Emergency calls combine electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can spill combustion gases if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Blocking the discharge pipe, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are mistakes we see far too often.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and a truck full of the likely parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. You get that answer before any work begins.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Leave clear space around a gas heater and never store flammable liquids nearby
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
